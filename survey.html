Our survey

What students at our school said

21 anonymous responses, collected on 25 September 2026, from students in MYP 1 to DP 2.

Why counts, not percentagesWe show results as “15 of 21”, not as percentages. With a group this small, percentages would make the results look more exact than they are. Some questions were answered by 20 students, not 21.

1 · Harmful — for whom?

Most of us think it harms other people

Our school (21 students)

15 of 21

say it mostly harms people their age — but only 5 of 21 say it mostly harms them.

US teens (Pew Research Center, 2024 survey)

48%

say it mostly harms people their age — but only 14% say it mostly harms them.

Same gap, much bigger survey. Psychologists have a name for it: the third-person effect — we tend to think media affects other people more than it affects us (Davison, 1983). That’s why this site is about noticing the hooks in your own feed.

2 · What keeps us on

What kept you on longer than you planned?

20 students answered and could tick more than one.

The top four — Shorts, Reels, the endless feed and autoplay — all work the same way: they take away the natural moment to stop. That trick is called no stopping cues.

About this survey

21 students chose to answer, anonymously, on 25 September 2026, from MYP 1 to DP 2. It’s a snapshot of what some of us think — not the whole school, and not proof that social media causes anything.

The comparison figures come from Pew Research Center’s survey of US teens aged 13 to 17, carried out in autumn 2024. Full details are in our sources list.

Hooked by Design

A Service as Action project by Avyukt Aggarwal, Neal Nikhil Suman, Ashvathh Sinnha and Atharva Kushwaha (MYP 4A).

No ads, no accounts, no tracking. Not affiliated with Instagram, Meta, YouTube or Google; their names are used only to describe their features. App features change often — last checked October 2026.
