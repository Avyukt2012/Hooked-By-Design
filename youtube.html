1

Shorts

The tricks behind it

  • 01Variable rewards
  • 02No stopping cues
  • 04Personalisation

Short, vertical and endless: swipe up and there’s always another. In June 2025, YouTube said Shorts were averaging over 200 billion views a day.

15 of 20students said Shorts kept them on longer than they planned — the top answer in our survey. Five asked us how to get rid of Shorts or fix their feed.

Take control: set a Shorts feed limit

  1. Tap You (your profile picture), then Settings.
  2. Tap Time management, then Shorts feed limit.
  3. Pick a time, from 15 minutes to 2 hours (this arrived in October 2025). When you reach it, YouTube pauses your Shorts scrolling with a reminder. You can dismiss it — it’s a speed bump, not a wall.

Want Shorts gone from Home?

Since April 2026 you can set the limit to 0 minutes. That removes Shorts from your Home page. It’s still rolling out, so you might not see the option yet — and single Shorts can still turn up in search and subscriptions.

2

Autoplay and Up next

The tricks behind it

  • 02No stopping cues
  • 08Unfinished tasks

When a video ends, the next one starts on its own after a short countdown. There’s never a natural moment to stop.

11 of 21students know how to turn autoplay off but haven’t. 5 have, and 5 don’t know how.

Try to beat the autoplay countdown

Take control: switch autoplay off

On the player

  1. Open any video.
  2. Tap the autoplay switch at the top of the player so it shows off.

Or in settings

  1. You → Settings → Autoplay.
  2. Turn off Autoplay next video.

TeensAutoplay has been off by default for 13–17-year-olds since 2021. If it’s on for you, it’s worth checking the switch.

3

Recommendations

The trick behind it

  • 04Personalisation

Home and Up next are built from what you’ve watched before. In 2018, YouTube said about 70% of watch time came from its recommendations.

6 of 20students said recommendations kept them on longer than they planned.

How the algorithm decides

Take control: steer what it suggests

Pause or clear your watch history

  1. Tap You → Settings → Manage all history.
  2. To pause: open Controls and stop saving the videos you watch. To clear: tap Delete and choose a time range.

Good to knowIf your watch history is off and empty, YouTube stops showing recommended videos on Home.

Tell it what you don’t want

  1. Tap ⋮ next to a video on Home.
  2. Choose Not interested, or Don’t recommend channel to stop seeing that channel.
4

Notifications and the bell

The trick behind it

  • 05Triggers

Every new upload can buzz your phone. Creators even ask you to “hit the bell” — because each notification is a reason to come back.

16 of 21students had opened an app because of a notification and stayed longer than they meant to.

Take control: fewer buzzes

For most channels

  1. On a channel you subscribe to, tap the bell next to Subscribed.
  2. Choose None. Keep All only for the few channels you really want.

Get them once a day instead

  1. You → Settings → Notifications.
  2. Switch off the kinds you don’t need, or turn on Scheduled digest to get them all together, once a day, at a time you choose.
5

Take-a-break and bedtime reminders

What it does

  • 02Puts a stopping cue back

These two reminders put back the stopping points that autoplay and endless feeds take away. They don’t lock anything — they just give you a moment to decide.

Take control: turn both on

  1. You → Settings → Time management.
  2. Turn on Remind me to take a break and choose how often.
  3. Turn on Remind me when it’s bedtime and choose a start and end time.

TeensBoth are on by default for 13–17-year-olds.

You asked

How does the algorithm decide what I see?

Students asked us this, so here’s the short version.

  1. It collects signalsWhat you watch and for how long, what you skip, like, search for and subscribe to — plus what people with similar taste watched.
  2. It makes a shortlistFrom millions of videos, it picks a few hundred you might watch.
  3. It ranks themIt predicts which ones you’re most likely to click and keep watching, and puts those at the top.
  4. It learns from youWhatever you do next becomes a new signal. That loop is why your feed gets more “you” the more you watch.

Based on how YouTube’s own engineers described their system (Covington and others, 2016).

The key idea: it’s very good at predicting what you’ll watch next. That isn’t always what you’d pick if you stopped to think — which is why the settings on this page are worth knowing.

      You asked

      Can I get rid of the ads?

      Two students asked us this. Honest answer: inside the app, the only official way is to pay for YouTube Premium. Ads are how YouTube makes its money — which is exactly why it wants so much of your time.
